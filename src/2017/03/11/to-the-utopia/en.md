Recently, after seeing and hearing some things, I've had some thoughts.

## A Discussion on Zhihu

A few days ago, I [left a comment under an answer on Zhihu](https://www.zhihu.com/question/47647183/answer/150720951), and for the past few days I've been getting likes and replies every day. It was originally just casual, but this really has been the most interaction I've ever encountered on Zhihu. About Zhihu—or rather, about society as a whole—I gained a little more understanding. When the woods get big, there really are all kinds of birds. If I had to sum up how I feel, it would be something like this:

- So many people think in [black-and-white terms](https://zh.wikipedia.org/zh/%E5%81%87%E5%85%A9%E9%9B%A3%E6%8E%A8%E7%90%86); it's basically impossible to have a proper discussion with them. At first I felt that not replying made it look like I couldn't argue back, but after a while I realized replying is exhausting.
- I used to think that every graduate of a certain school was admirable. It turns out that's not the case.
- I've come to understand even more deeply that behind everything lies interest.
- As beneficiaries of vested interests, they are all stingy; and they use that same stinginess to judge others, assuming that if others were also beneficiaries of vested interests, they would think the same way.
- Many people's cognition and worldview completely conflict with mine. For example, some people think class solidification—things like the son of a dragon being a dragon, the son of a phoenix being a phoenix—is a good thing, and that some people simply ought to be exploited.
  I think this society is especially tragic.

## Shared Bikes

This afternoon, I went out for a bit. Maybe because it happened to be the time when everyone was heading out, I saw more shared bikes than usual. But this short stretch of road left me feeling heavy-hearted.

I saw three ofo bikes with their license plates damaged. It was obvious someone had claimed them for personal use, and one of them I even saw being ridden.

I reported them one by one in the app, but while reporting them I knew in my heart that it was useless: my reports didn't include license plate numbers, only a location and photos. Even if someone came to deal with it, who knows where the bike would have gone by then.

This is theft, plain and simple, right? Maybe I should report it to the police, but I also know that would be useless: I'm not the owner, the police's efficiency is rather lackluster, and the value of the case is so low. In the end, even without my reporting it, the police must already know about these things—yet they still haven't dealt with them.

There was also plenty of damage to the bikes. I hadn't seen any missing arms or legs, so to speak, but I saw quite a few with warped rims, bent front ends, and broken wheel locks. Out of a sense of civic-mindedness, I reported those too. And this doesn't only mean ofo bikes; it includes Mobike as well.

Come to think of it, shared bikes have only been around for a few months, right? If this trend continues, I really don't know what the final outcome for shared bikes will be, and I'm quite worried. I really like this kind of convenience, and I do my best to help maintain it, hoping I can keep using it. But pessimistically, I feel that this thing seems to be dying at the hands of petty people who exploit loopholes and deliberately or unintentionally damage things (including users who simply don't use them with care).

I think this society is especially tragic.

## Utopia

Recently, while memorizing vocabulary, I happened to come across the word utopia—a dreamland.

Actually, I'm quite a realist. I know very clearly that kind of ideal state is impossible for a very, very long time to come. I'm careful with my wording: "a very, very long time." But I won't remove that qualifier, even if I admit it approaches infinity.

I wouldn't dare describe myself directly as someone who deeply understands the greater good, but I do have profound views of my own about what is right and wrong, good and bad, worth pursuing or not. I know what I like, and what I absolutely detest. What I deeply loathe, I deeply loathe—there isn't even a little room in my heart to forgive it.

Especially certain self-styled political factions, and the people who join them: sycophantic, opportunistic, hypocritical. Sometimes I even feel nauseated just watching how such people treat their superiors and how they treat their colleagues.

So I once thought: if possible, why not select some people and found a dreamland, so we wouldn't have to live together with these low-quality, low-morality people. As I said earlier, I know that's impossible. But I still think about how to make it possible, or at least how to get closer to it.

I even had passing thoughts of something as extreme as ethnic cleansing—of course, that itself goes against my values. Just a thought.

Perhaps the easiest thing to get close to is a harsh legal system. Using Legalist ideas to deal with scoundrels is probably best—for example, making theft punishable by death outright.

Anyway, it's just fantasy.

But if you don't even dare to dream, if you've given up on dreaming altogether, don't you think you're already dead—just waiting a few more years to be buried?
