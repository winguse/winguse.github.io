Some recent experiences gave me a lot to think about.

## A discussion on Zhihu

A few days ago I [left a comment under a Zhihu answer](https://www.zhihu.com/question/47647183/answer/150720951), and for days I kept receiving likes and replies. It was not intentional, but this was the most interaction I have ever had on Zhihu. I gained a bit more understanding of Zhihu—and society in general. When a community gets large, all kinds of people appear. My takeaways:

- Many people think in [black-and-white fallacies](https://zh.wikipedia.org/zh/%E5%81%87%E5%85%A9%E9%9B%A3%E6%8E%A8%E7%90%86), making meaningful discussion almost impossible. At first I felt that not replying looked like I had no rebuttal; later I realized replying all the time is exhausting.
- I used to think graduates from certain elite schools were all admirable. That is clearly not true.
- I now feel even more strongly that interests drive almost everything.
- Those who already benefit from a system are often stingy, and they measure everyone else with the same mindset—assuming others would behave the same way if they also had privilege.
- Many people's cognition and worldview conflict with mine. Some even think class solidification is good, that "the children of dragons remain dragons," and that some people are meant to be exploited.
  I find that deeply tragic.

## Bike sharing

This afternoon I went out, probably around peak outing hours, so I saw more shared bikes than usual. That short trip made me heavy-hearted.

I saw three yellow shared bikes with damaged license plates; clearly someone had effectively claimed them for private use. I even saw one being ridden.

I reported each one in the app. But while reporting, I also knew it was probably not very useful: without plate numbers, only location and photos, by the time someone handles it, the bike could be anywhere.

This is theft, no doubt. Maybe I should report it to police, but I also know that likely would not help much: I am not the property owner, enforcement efficiency is often limited, and case value is low. Even without my report, police probably already know this exists, yet little happens.

Vehicle damage is also common. I did not see fully broken frames, but I saw deformed rims, bent handlebars, and damaged locks. I reported those too. This is not only for Ofo, but also Mobike.

Bike sharing has only existed for a short time. If this trend continues, I do not know what its ending will be. I really like this convenience and try to protect it, hoping I can keep using it. But pessimistically, it seems to be dying from freeloaders and people who intentionally or carelessly damage public property.

I find that deeply tragic.

## Utopia

Recently while memorizing words, I came across "utopia" again—an ideal nation.

I am actually a practical person. I know that ideal state is impossible for a very, very long time. I choose my wording carefully: "very, very long." I will not remove that qualifier, even if I admit it may approach infinity.

I would not call myself exceptionally noble, but I do have deep convictions about right and wrong, good and bad, and what I pursue. I know what I love and what I strongly detest. What I detest, I detest completely—there is almost no room for forgiveness in my mind.

Especially certain self-righteous political groups and people who join them—flattery upward, opportunism, hypocrisy. Sometimes just seeing how they treat superiors versus peers feels disgusting.

So I once imagined: if possible, maybe select a group of people and establish a dream nation, so we would not have to live together with those who are low in ethics and civility. As I said, I know this is unrealistic. But I still think about how to get closer to such a state.

I even briefly thought of extreme ideas like ethnic cleansing—though that itself violates my own values. Just a passing thought.

Maybe the closest practical route is strict rule of law. Perhaps governing malicious behavior with harsh legalism would be effective, for example very severe punishment for theft.

Anyway, this is mostly fantasy.

But if someone no longer dares to dream and even gives up dreaming, are they not already dead—just waiting a few more years to be buried?
