---
title: "Information Should Flow Freely"
date: 2017-03-27 09:35:00 +0800
---

My view on information is simple: it should flow freely. So I strongly dislike things that block information flow, for example:

1. WeChat public accounts: no RSS, plus anti-crawling everywhere. In fact, Tencent products often follow this stingy style—build closed loops and keep all value in-house. Want to crawl data? No way. Want to export your own data? Also no (encrypted formats that can only be re-imported to the same app do not count). It is deeply unpleasant.
2. Zhihu Live: prices increase after an event ends. That blocks already-created knowledge from spreading. In essence, later distribution has near-zero production cost, and some knowledge even depreciates over time. Many Q&A products do similar things. In contrast, Weibo Q&A at least keeps spectator pricing at 1 RMB, which is worth acknowledging.

I know people will challenge this view: those products are commercial and have no obligation to provide such services. I agree in principle. The world is big, everyone has their likes and dislikes, and I simply dislike these choices. But I also have my own values and principles. At a broader level, I think they are not fulfilling their public responsibility: much of this information is publicly published, yet their products intentionally restrict the circulation of that public information.

I am not saying this must be free either. Even the GPL mentions redistribution costs:

> URL: https://www.gnu.org/licenses/gpl-2.0.txt
> In section 3.b:
>
> … for a charge no more than your cost of physically performing source distribution …

For commercial companies, charging for service is completely fine. What I criticize is refusing to do it at all, or deliberately restricting it. Sometimes you can tell a company's attitude toward information flow just by looking at its `robots.txt`. Many domestic companies seem to lack that spirit of openness.

Finally, some people say, "If you do not like it, just do not use it." My response: first, I do not normalize coercive product behavior; second, maybe you have not seen enough examples. Look at benchmark companies: Medium provides RSS, and Google and Facebook provide RSS and user data export options.

Anyway, you can disagree with me. I am not asking for agreement—this is simply my value system.

That's all.
