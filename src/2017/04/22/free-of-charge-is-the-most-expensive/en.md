---
title: "What Is Free Is the Most Expensive; You Can't Rely on Commercial Companies"
date: 2017-04-22 11:58:00 +0800
---

Recently I did something: I wrote a crawler to fetch content from WeChat official accounts. My motivation for doing this was that I no longer wanted to waste time on fragmented reading in WeChat. There are too many pain points in reading inside WeChat:

1. For a public account, once you open it you have to read through all unread items.
2. Ordering is done at the account level as a whole, so selective reading is hard; each time you can only see the latest item from a single account.
3. It supports favorites, but the data is not structured and cannot be tagged.
4. Its closed data policy means you can forget about Tencent letting you export anything. Even when export is possible, it's in an encrypted proprietary format, hypocritically described as protecting users.

So now what I do is turn the data into an RSS Feed. I have to admit that this product from the beginning of Web 2.0 has its own irreplaceable advancement.

Another thing happened recently: tipping on iOS WeChat official account articles was required to use Apple's IAP (In App Purchase), and WeChat responded by taking a go-down-fighting approach and directly cutting the feature. If you read Apple's terms carefully, no one could fail to feel that this was simply Apple's overbearing interpretation of the rules: tipping does not purchase any substantive function or value-added service, yet Apple still insists on taking a cut.

On this matter, I lean toward WeChat, though I still don't really want to speak for it, for the same reason as above: WeChat itself is also a particularly disgusting closed ecosystem. It monopolizes our communication and social relationships, and in many ways goes even further than Apple did this time.

As a commercial company, there is nothing wrong with pursuing profit. It's just that sometimes the way they go about it is too ugly. The internet originated in openness, yet it has been restricted again and again; that spirit is unacceptable to betray.

Nothing is more important than data. That's also why I wrote that crawler. When it comes to data openness, domestic companies all seem to share this same habit: data within their own ecosystems is always restricted in all sorts of ways, showing very little internet spirit. By contrast, Google / Facebook both provide users with at least some export capability, and are committed to establishing open format standards. But Google has also done things like shutting down Google Reader, so no commercial company can be relied upon. As individuals, we should clearly realize this: so-called cloud computing and large commercial companies are not dependable. In this era, what is free is the most expensive.

Similarly, I recently discovered that Facebook Moves' export function has a bug, and the data I export is incomplete. The good news is that this thing provides a usable open API, so my next toy to tinker with is ready. Another example is my personal finances. I've tried quite a few bookkeeping apps, but none of them made me feel comfortable enough to use or were good enough, so I also plan to write something to fetch bank data and make it easy to tag. Considering data security, it's probably better for these things to run locally and only use basic cloud storage infrastructure for backups. Simply put, at the very least, make sure you can export your own data from commercial companies in a structured way.

Hmm, I've also been a bit unhappy with Wordpress lately, and I plan to migrate fully to Github Pages.

Well, it's a good thing I'm a programmer, so I can DIY all of this. Of course, doing these things is also very expensive, after all, my salary sets the price 😂.

What is free is the most expensive; you can't rely on commercial companies.
