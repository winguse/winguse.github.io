Today Tyr shared with us [some of his thoughts on Bitcoin](http://mp.weixin.qq.com/s/Znu7caKS11xdy_K2GEamjA). Although I had read [that original 9-page whitepaper](https://bitcoin.org/bitcoin.pdf) by Satoshi Nakamoto long ago, I had still only skimmed it, and had not thought through some details very well, such as:

- Why is one chain block 1 MB?
- Why is the total supply 21 M?
- Why use the hash of the public key rather than the public key itself?

So yes, I really should learn from Tyr and put more thought into these things.

By the way, in the afternoon I also looked at Ethereum materials, and there is really a huge amount of material out there. I keep thinking that designing a forward-looking, planet-scale distributed consensus system — whether Bitcoin or Ethereum — is truly the work of astonishing genius.

But **given my current understanding**, I still think investment in blockchain is not rational at the moment. It is still a solution with many limitations, and it will not become a kind of high-frequency application; perhaps it is only a useful supplement to today’s centralized solutions. For example, here is one of my current doubts, and what I consider a problem with blockchain: as blockchain transaction data keeps growing over time, miners will inevitably face very high requirements for computing power, networking, and storage. Ordinary people will not be able to participate. In that case, what is the difference between this and a centralized system formed by a few oligarchs? Under such conditions, how can we solve issues such as whether miner nodes remain loyal, rising transaction fees, the wasted computing power of unlucky miners, and the waste of storing useless historical data? I think that if we are talking about a **planet-scale distributed consensus system**, then considering its use over hundreds of years and many generations is not excessive at all. Of course, speculation and harvesting retail investors are another matter.
